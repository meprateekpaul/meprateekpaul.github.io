## 3. Required Report Sections and Technical Tasks

Your formal report must be structured into the following five technical sections:

### Section 1: Sequence Identification & Statistical Evidence (25 Marks)
1. Execute a `blastp` search against the NCBI non-redundant protein database (`nr`).
2. Provide a tabulated summary of your top 3 database hits:
   - Hit Description & Organism
   - Database Accession Number (RefSeq / UniProt)
   - Max Bit Score & Expectation value ($E$-value)
   - Query Coverage (%) & Percentage Identity (%)
3. Unambiguously declare the identity of your protein:
   - **Protein Name**, **Gene Symbol**, and **Source Organism (Taxonomy)**.
   - Justify your conclusion using Karlin–Altschul statistics (explain why the $E$-value rules out random chance).

### Section 2: Pairwise Alignment & Conservation Analysis (20 Marks)
1. Retrieve the canonical reference protein sequence for your top hit from UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot.
2. Execute a pairwise alignment between your query and the reference sequence using EMBOSS `needle` (global Needleman–Wunsch) or `water` (local Smith–Waterman) at EMBL-EBI.
3. Report the alignment parameters used (Matrix: BLOSUM62, Gap Open: 10, Gap Extend: 0.5).
4. State the exact number and percentage of:
   - Identical positions (`|`)
   - Conservative substitutions (`:`)
   - Semi-conservative substitutions (`.`)
   - Gaps introduced (`-`)
5. Discuss any sequence discrepancies (e.g., initiator Met clipping, point mutations, signal peptide cleavage, or tag sequences).

### Section 3: Physicochemical Characterisation (20 Marks)
Submit your sequence to Expasy **ProtParam** and record:
1. Number of amino acids and calculated theoretical Molecular Weight (in Daltons / kDa).
2. Theoretical Isoelectric Point (pI) and net charge at physiological pH (7.4).
3. Total number of negatively charged (Asp + Glu) and positively charged (Arg + Lys) residues.
4. Molar extinction coefficient at 280 nm ($\epsilon_{280}$ in $\text{M}^{-1}\text{cm}^{-1}$) assuming all cysteines form cystines vs all cysteines reduced.
5. Instability Index (state whether predicted stable or unstable in vitro, $<40$ vs $>40$).
6. Aliphatic Index and Grand Average of Hydropathicity (GRAVY) score.
7. Generate a Kyte–Doolittle hydropathy plot via ProtScale (window size 9 and window size 19); state whether the protein is globally soluble or possesses putative transmembrane segments.

### Section 4: Motifs, Patterns & Secondary Structure Prediction (20 Marks)
1. Scan your sequence against the **PROSITE** and **InterPro** databases.
   - List all detected sequence motifs, signatures, or Pfam domains with start and end residue coordinates.
   - Identify critical catalytic residues, metal coordination centers, or disulfide bridges documented in the literature for these motifs.
2. Predict the secondary structure using **GOR IV** (NPS@ server) or **PSIPRED**.
   - Report the predicted percentage content of Alpha-Helix ($H$), Extended Beta-Strand ($E$), and Random Coil ($C$).
   - Identify the primary structural folding class (All-$\alpha$, All-$\beta$, $\alpha/\beta$, or $\alpha+\beta$).

### Section 5: 3D Structure & Molecular Visualisation (15 Marks)
1. Locate the experimental crystal, NMR, or cryo-EM structure of your protein (or its closest homologue) in the **RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB)**.
   - Report the 4-character PDB ID, experimental determination method, and resolution (in $\text{\AA}$).
   - (If no experimental structure exists, provide the AlphaFold DB accession and average pLDDT score).
2. Open the structure in **VMD**, **PyMOL**, or **Mol*** (RCSB in-browser).
3. Generate and embed one publication-quality rendered 3D figure showing:
   - Cartoon representation colored by secondary structure or chain.
   - Key functional active site residues, catalytic triads, or prosthetic groups highlighted in stick/licorice representation with residue labels.
4. Provide a 3-line descriptive caption detailing the structural fold and active site geometry.
