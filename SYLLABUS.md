## Official University Syllabus

### Theory

1. **Bioinformatics and Internet:**  
   Internet Basics, FTP, www, connecting to Internet, Electronic mail, internet resources.
2. **Information Retrieval from Biological Databases:**  
   Integrated information Retrieval (Entrez System), Retrieving database entries.
3. **The NCBI data model:**  
   Introduction, Seq-id, Sequence, collection of sequence, annotation of sequence, Describing sequence.
4. **GenBank Sequence Database:**  
   Introduction to structure, Primary and secondary database, Format vs Content: Computer vs. Human, Databases, Genbank Flat file, GCG.
5. **Sequence Alignment And Database Searching:**  
   Introduction, Evolutionary Basis of Sequence Alignment, Optimal alignment method, Substitution Score and Gap Penalty, Statistical Significance of Alignment Database similarity searching, FASTA, BLAST, Database searching Artifacts, Position Specific Scoring Matrices.
6. **Multiple Sequence Alignment:**  
   What is MSA, Structural or Evolutionary Alignment, how to align Sequences, Tools.
7. **Phylogenetic Analysis:**  
   Fundamental of Phylogenetic model, Tree interpretation - Paralogues and orthologues, Tree building and tree evaluation, Phylogenetic software.
8. **Predictive Method using Nucleotide Sequence:**  
   Introduction, Marking repetitive DNA, Database search, Codon bias detection, detecting functional site in DNA.
9. **Predictive Method using Protein Sequence:**  
   Protein identification based on composition, Physical properties based on sequence, Motif and pattern, Secondary structure and folding classes, specialized structure or features, Tertiary structures.
10. **Structure Database:**  
    Introduction to Structure, PDB, MMDB, Structure file format, visualizing structure information, Structure viewers, structure similarity searching, Advanced structure modeling.
11. **Comparative Genome Analysis:**  
    Introduction, application, genome analysis and annotation.

## Learning Outcomes (LO1–LO8)

By the conclusion of this course, students will be able to:
- **LO1:** Describe what kinds of biological data exist, where each is stored, and how the major databases link to one another.
- **LO2:** Retrieve nucleotide, protein, structure, and literature records using targeted Entrez queries, and parse every section of a GenBank flat file.
- **LO3:** Explain global, local, and heuristic alignment, compute optimal dynamic programming matrices by hand, and justify substitution matrices and gap penalties.
- **LO4:** Perform BLAST and FASTA searches, evaluating hits by E-value, bit score, identity, coverage, and masking artifacts.
- **LO5:** Build multiple sequence alignments and phylogenetic trees, correctly interpreting orthology, paralogy, and bootstrap support.
- **LO6:** Predict sequence-based features: ORFs, codon usage bias, and repeats in DNA; composition, physicochemical properties, motifs, and secondary structure in proteins.
- **LO7:** Query the PDB/MMDB, parse structure files (ATOM, HETATM, SEQRES), and render 3D visual representations.
- **LO8:** Explain comparative genomics workflows, synteny, genome annotation, and evolutionary conservation.
